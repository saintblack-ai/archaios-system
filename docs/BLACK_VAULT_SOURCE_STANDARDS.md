# Black Vault Source Standards

## Core rule

Every substantive claim needs traceable provenance. A source can inform a hypothesis without validating it; a conclusion must state what the cited material actually supports and what remains uncertain.

## Source record requirements

Each source record should include, where applicable:

- stable citation: author/creator, title, publisher or archive, date, edition/identifier, and location or durable URL;
- source type, original language and translation/edition if relevant;
- access date for mutable web material and a preservation reference or archive copy when permitted;
- exact locator: page, chapter, timestamp, collection box/folder, or equivalent;
- rights/access note and classification;
- short relevance note describing which claim it bears on.

Never store access tokens, passwords, private sharing links, or copied restricted content in citations or repository documentation.

## Evidence tiers

| Tier | Evidence | Normal use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Primary documents, original artifacts/data, official records, direct contemporaneous accounts | Establish direct facts, with provenance limits stated. |
| B | Peer-reviewed scholarship, critical editions, reputable academic/reference works | Context, interpretation, and corroboration. |
| C | Credible investigative reporting, expert analysis, institutional secondary records | Leads and contextual synthesis; corroborate important claims. |
| D | Popular works, unsourced compilations, oral accounts, speculative media, AI-generated text | Discovery leads only unless independently verified. |

No tier removes the need to check authenticity, date, scope, translation, bias, and conflicts of interest. Extraordinary claims require independently traceable, high-quality support and clear counterevidence review.

## Claim handling

- Write claims atomically and distinguish quotation, observation, inference, and speculation.
- Link each claim to one or more sources and assign claim-level confidence where claims materially differ.
- Record meaningful counterevidence, scholarly disagreement, provenance gaps, and known alternative explanations.
- Do not use an AI-generated summary as a source. It may assist drafting only and must cite underlying material.
- Do not turn recurring internet assertions, mythology, esoteric interpretation, or forecasting into factual claims without marking their evidentiary status.

## Review and publication standard

Before `published`, confirm that sources are locatable, citations support the stated claim, classification is correct, confidence is current, and the summary does not overstate the evidence. For sensitive or high-impact entries, require a second reviewer and retain the review decision.
